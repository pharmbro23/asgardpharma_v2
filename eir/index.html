The problem

Most counting apps send your photos somewhere else.

Many pill-counting apps use your phone's camera, then upload every photo to a third-party AI service to do the counting. Images of your counting tray, and sometimes a patient's label caught in the corner of the frame, leave the pharmacy and are processed on servers you don't control, often outside Canada.

Patient confidentiality

One stray prescription label in the frame puts a patient's name and medication on someone else's server.

Data leaves Canada

Large AI providers commonly process requests on servers abroad, outside the reach of Canadian privacy protections.

Costs that never stop

Monthly subscriptions and per-photo AI credits add up to hundreds of dollars a year, for every pharmacy.

How it works

Three steps. Zero uploads.

1

2

3

Verify and dispense

Each detected pill is highlighted, so you can confirm the count at a glance before dispensing.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Does Eir send my photos anywhere?

No. Counting happens entirely on your phone. Photos are never sent to Asgard or any third party unless you choose to join the improvement program.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. The counting model runs on the device, so Eir works without Wi-Fi or mobile data.

What is the improvement program?

An optional way to help us train better models by sharing some of your counting photos with Asgard. It is off by default, you can leave at any time, and nothing is shared unless you turn it on.

Is Eir a subscription?

No. You pay once for the app. There are no monthly fees and no per-count AI credits.

Does Eir replace pharmacist verification?

No. Eir is a counting aid. It highlights every pill it detects so you can check the count quickly, but the pharmacist remains responsible for verifying each prescription.

Which phones will Eir support?

Supported devices will be announced at launch. Join the early access list and we will let you know.
